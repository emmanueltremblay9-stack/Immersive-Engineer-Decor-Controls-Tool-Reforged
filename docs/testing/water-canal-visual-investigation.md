The existing nonce-guarded native client runner creates its own fresh integrated world. After the existing lamp and fluidlogging regressions, it captures oblique and overhead views of six scenes: waterlogged stairs, dry stairs (empty canal so adjacent water cannot fill the control), waterlogged low catwalk, waterlogged raised catwalk, and stairs with both railings in opposite orientations. The input isolation screen renders no background or blur.

The proof records client block/fluid states and direct fluid tessellation. The gate requires upper fluid quads in wet cases and none in the dry control, plus matching screenshot names, PNG headers and dimensions. These checks do not prove final pixel visibility. Review the twelve PNGs in the `water-canal-scenes` CI artifact. Both longitudinal stair orientations are covered, with closer overhead views; this is not an exact reconstruction of the user's world or shader/resource-pack environment.

No installed JAR, Prism instance or user world is read or modified. Passive accumulator behavior and all production block/fluid logic remain unchanged. No release or installation is part of this investigation.

## Follow-up: adjacent flow versus a stored source

The user pictures show blue water ending immediately beside gray stair treads, between brick boundaries and visible stair rails. The prior wet scenes explicitly installed a source inside the stair; their blue surface covers the treads. The prior dry control emptied the whole canal, so it did not cover the pictured blue/gray boundary. Neither the screenshot colors nor the tooltip reveal the targeted block's waterlogged property or the adjacent fluid level. Perspective prevents an absolute fluid-height measurement from those pixels.

The new pair keeps the same brick geometry, railings, orientation and cameras, starts one source three blocks upstream, and compares an initially dry stair with a stair storing a source. Fluid ticks form the intervening stream naturally. The stored source may influence that stream; the actual neighboring fluid state/source flag/height is therefore recorded separately in both scenes, rather than assuming identical settled flow. Normal wet-cell surface height is 8/9 of a block, above the stair's 4/16 and12/16 treads; a dry cell has no fluid surface to render. Low/raised catwalks, both longitudinal stair slopes and rails remain covered by the existing scenes.

Source inspection confirms naturally flowing water is not silently converted into a stored source by the container API. That explains a possible blue-water/gray-stair boundary under the current contract, but does not establish that the user stair was dry or invalidate the reported behavior. If the flowing-water pair matches the appearance without reproducing an occupied-cell rendering failure, the smallest discriminating next datum is one F3 screenshot aimed at the exact affected stair after the successful filling action, showing `waterlogged` (and `fluid_cell`) properties. No installed-JAR identity, hash or instance access is needed for that question.

First flowing-water evidence (`f433f182`, CI `38680647684`) captured both states: a dry stair beside level-6 water (height 0.6666667) stays gray; a stored source (height 0.8888889) beside level-7 water (height 0.7777778) is visibly blue over the treads. An independent reviewer inspected all four new PNGs and confirmed this distinction. This reproduces a similar appearance for an empty cell, not a missing render for an occupied one, and does not determine the user's actual state.

That run also demonstrated a diagnostic false negative: a sloped liquid top had eight vertices but zero perfectly horizontal quads. The gate now counts upper surfaces with four vertices above half-height and nonzero projected XZ area. Vertical side quads project zero area and cannot substitute for a top; the old horizontal count remains in the JSON for inspection. Dedicated checks accept the sloped case and reject side-only/missing-top evidence. The production renderer remains unchanged.

## Natural flow distance sweep

After preserving the completed four-profile qualification on commit93e9e866 (both event CI runs green), a separate observation-only pass starts one fixed source and places a fresh initially dry east-facing stair at distances1..8 in independent scenes. No bucket, placeLiquid call or WATERLOGGED assignment is used. An unobstructed channel establishes the seven-block reach and the eighth position beyond it. A fixed overview camera and a translated close camera keep their respective angles constant. Each case records source/upstream/target/downstream fluid state, amount, height, actual block state and every channel position, plus both rendered frames.

The natural-flow session has its own suite identity and completion marker; it cannot qualify lamp-mining tests that it does not run. The evidence gate accepts either observed passage or observed blockage, rejecting stale profiles, forced fills, incomplete distances, moved sources, incorrect target coordinates, unstable states or changed camera angles. It does not claim pixel correctness: each distance must be visually reviewed. The Windows runner can validate/reuse completed evidence in an explicitly owned build directory with -ResumeDirectory, refusing to overwrite incomplete profiles.

Preliminary native and Embeddium client observations show the current stops at dry stairs at reachable distances1..7; distance8 is outside the free-channel reach. The stair and downstream cells remain empty in those client snapshots and the inspected frames show exposed gray treads. This is distinct from the previous stored-source rendering tests. Authoritative server/client agreement is required before the final mechanical classification; no production behavior has been changed by this diagnostic addition.

The final fixture additionally takes a channel snapshot on the integrated server thread, after at least100 actual server ticks, and requires equality with the stabilized client channel before capture. Both tick measurements are retained with explicit names. Separate upstream/target/downstream summaries must equal their channel entries, and target coordinates must equal source-minus-distance. The local preliminary batch was completed before this strengthening; its original client-only observations remain retained rather than being rewritten as authoritative evidence. Exact-head CI runs the strengthened fixture.


## Authorized natural water passage through steel catwalk stairs

The follow-up is limited to the existing steel catwalk stair registration. Other native cells, vanilla blocks, lava and IE creosote retain their source-only behavior. A stair stores transient water separately from its legacy WATERLOGGED source: water_flow=0 is empty,1..7 are horizontal amounts,8..15 encode falling amounts8..1. Old states default to0. WATERLOGGED and non-water stored cells take precedence.

Vanilla computes water levels, tick delays and spread. A targeted FlowingFluid tick adaptation preserves the stair when a transient level changes or disappears. Natural water is not promoted into a permanent stored source, including between neighboring sources; intentional bucket/legacy sources retain their old behavior. Flow cannot fill a bucket. A water bucket may intentionally replace transient water with a source and clears the old transient level; source pickup also clears it.

The v2 integrated-world qualification uses a fixed source and independent dry east-facing stairs at distances1..8, a free-channel control and a separately identified existing-source-cell control. It requires exact vanilla amounts8..1 through distance7, dry distance8, no additional natural sources, authoritative server/client agreement and complete recession after upstream source removal. The prefilled control must remain a source after upstream removal. Overview, close and post-removal screenshots are retained for actual pixel review. These are acceptance checks for the authorized mechanical change, unlike the earlier observation-only v1 gates.
