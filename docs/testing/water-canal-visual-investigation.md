# Water canal visual investigation

User screenshots show a brick canal terminating at steel catwalk stairs, with visible upstream water and gray exposed treads. Both supplied images were inspected. This change adds evidence collection; it does not yet establish a production defect or claim a fix.

The existing nonce-guarded native client runner creates its own fresh integrated world. After the existing lamp and fluidlogging regressions, it captures oblique and overhead views of six scenes: waterlogged stairs, dry stairs (empty canal so adjacent water cannot fill the control), waterlogged low catwalk, waterlogged raised catwalk, and stairs with both railings in opposite orientations. Brick sides, floor, upstream sources and an open top form a bounded synthetic canal. Creative flight holds the camera still; each teleport must synchronize before waiting for rendered frames. The input isolation screen renders no background or blur.

The proof records client block/fluid states and direct fluid tessellation. The gate requires upper fluid quads in wet cases and none in the dry control, plus matching screenshot names, PNG headers and dimensions. These checks do not prove final pixel visibility. Review the twelve PNGs in the `water-canal-scenes` CI artifact. Both longitudinal stair orientations are covered, with closer overhead views; this is not an exact reconstruction of the user's world or shader/resource-pack environment.

No installed JAR, Prism instance or user world is read or modified. Passive accumulator behavior and all production block/fluid logic remain unchanged. No release or installation is part of this investigation.

## Follow-up: adjacent flow versus a stored source

The user pictures show blue water ending immediately beside gray stair treads, between brick boundaries and visible stair rails. The prior wet scenes explicitly installed a source inside the stair; their blue surface covers the treads. The prior dry control emptied the whole canal, so it did not cover the pictured blue/gray boundary. Neither the screenshot colors nor the tooltip reveal the targeted block's waterlogged property or the adjacent fluid level. Perspective prevents an absolute fluid-height measurement from those pixels.

The new pair keeps the same brick geometry, railings, orientation and cameras, starts one source three blocks upstream, and compares an initially dry stair with a stair storing a source. Fluid ticks form the intervening stream naturally. The stored source may influence that stream; the actual neighboring fluid state/source flag/height is therefore recorded separately in both scenes, rather than assuming identical settled flow. Normal wet-cell surface height is 8/9 of a block, above the stair's 4/16 and12/16 treads; a dry cell has no fluid surface to render. Low/raised catwalks, both longitudinal stair slopes and rails remain covered by the existing scenes.

Source inspection confirms naturally flowing water is not silently converted into a stored source by the container API. That explains a possible blue-water/gray-stair boundary under the current contract, but does not establish that the user stair was dry or invalidate the reported behavior. If the flowing-water pair matches the appearance without reproducing an occupied-cell rendering failure, the smallest discriminating next datum is one F3 screenshot aimed at the exact affected stair after the successful filling action, showing `waterlogged` (and `fluid_cell`) properties. No installed-JAR identity, hash or instance access is needed for that question.
